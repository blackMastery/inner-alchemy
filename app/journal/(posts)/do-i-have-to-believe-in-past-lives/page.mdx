No. And if you're asking, you're probably a better candidate than you think.

People arrive at this work from very different directions. Some have a settled spiritual framework and
find the language natural. Others have none, are faintly embarrassed to be enquiring at all, and would
describe themselves as skeptical. That second group does not do worse. If anything, skeptics often have
the most striking sessions.

## Belief isn't the mechanism

The assumption behind the question is that belief is doing the work — that the material shows up because
you expect it to, and stays away if you don't.

That isn't how it goes in practice. What's actually required is much smaller and much more specific:
a willingness to say what comes without editing it on the way out.

That's it. Not conviction. Not faith in any particular model of consciousness. Just a few hours of
reporting what's there without grading it first.

Skeptical people are frequently good at exactly this, because they aren't invested in the material
meaning any particular thing. They're not steering toward a conclusion. They just describe what they
notice, plainly, and let it be strange.

## You don't have to decide what it was

During a session what arises may be another lifetime, a memory from this one, or something more
symbolic. You don't have to settle which — not during, and not afterwards.

You can hold it as memory. You can hold it as metaphor — the subconscious using narrative because that's
how it has always communicated, in dreams and everywhere else. You can hold it as imagination and still
take it seriously, in the way you'd take a dream that clearly meant something seriously.

> Whether you frame what arises as memory, metaphor, or imagination, the material is meaningful and the
> insights are yours.

Notice what stays constant across all three readings: the content came from you. It used your associations
and your history. It arrived in your own voice. Whatever metaphysics you attach to it afterwards, that
part doesn't change — and it's the part that does the work.

## What scepticism is genuinely useful for

Being skeptical about the frame is fine. Being skeptical about the practitioner is *sensible*, and I'd
rather you were.

Ask what the session involves, hour by hour. Ask what happens if it goes lightly. Ask what is and isn't
being claimed — this work is complementary, it does not diagnose or treat any condition, and it is not a
substitute for a doctor or a licensed therapist. Any answer that gets vague under direct questioning is
information.

The one thing scepticism shouldn't have to do is decide in advance what the experience will be. You can
book while genuinely unsure. Most people do.
