Most pages about this work don't answer this question, which is precisely why it deserves a direct one.

Underneath it is rarely curiosity. It's usually the worry that you will spend the money, block out most
of a day, arrive hopeful — and be the person it doesn't work for. That you'll lie there, feel nothing,
and have to make polite conversation about it afterwards.

Here is the honest version.

## Sometimes people stay in a lighter state

A small number of people stay in a lighter state on a first session. It happens. Pretending otherwise
would make this page useless.

When it does happen, the cause is almost always the same, and it's a little unfair: people try too hard
to do it right. They monitor themselves for whether it's working. They wonder whether what they're seeing
counts. That watching is itself an effort, and effort is the one thing the state doesn't tolerate — it's
the same reason you can't fall asleep by concentrating on falling asleep.

## "Lighter" is not "nothing"

This is the part that gets lost. A lighter state is not a failed session — it's a shallower one, and
shallow states still produce real material.

It also helps to know that the experience varies enormously between people even when it goes deep. For
some it's vivid and cinematic. For others it's a quiet knowing — no pictures at all, just a clear sense
of what's there. People in the second group often assume they're doing it wrong, right up until they
listen back to the recording and hear how specific they were.

> Whether you frame what arises as memory, metaphor, or imagination, the material is meaningful and the
> insights are yours.

## Half the day isn't hypnosis at all

Worth remembering when you're weighing the risk: roughly half of a session is conversation.

The first couple of hours are an interview — your life story and your question list, gone through
properly. That part is not incidental, and it isn't warm-up. It's where the session gets built, and it's
what makes the rest precise. Many people say afterwards that the interview alone was worth the day.

So the worst realistic case is not "nothing happened." It's "the regression was lighter than I hoped, and
I still spent two hours being asked better questions about my life than anyone has asked me in years."

## What happens if we get very little

I'll tell you plainly. I won't perform enthusiasm about a session that was thin, and I won't tell you
something profound happened if it didn't — that would be worse than the disappointment.

We'd talk about what we did get, what seemed to be in the way, and decide together on next steps,
including a second attempt at a reduced rate. You'd still leave with the complete recording.

## How to make it less likely

Nothing here is a trick, and none of it is a guarantee:

- Come rested, sober, and unhurried. Don't schedule anything after.
- Write your questions in advance, in your own words.
- Let go of grading yourself during the session. Say what comes, including the things that seem too
  ordinary or too odd to mention. Those are frequently the thread.
- Plan a quiet evening. Insights keep arriving for days, especially after a second listen.

If you're weighing this up, the discovery call exists for exactly this conversation. It's free, it's
fifteen minutes, and "I'm worried it won't work on me" is a completely normal thing to open with.
