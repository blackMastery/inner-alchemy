This is the question that stops most people from booking. It is rarely asked out loud, because it sounds
superstitious once you say it. It isn't. It's a reasonable thing to want to know before you let someone
guide your attention for several hours.

The short answer: no. You do not lose control, you are not asleep, and you cannot be made to do or say
anything you don't want to.

## Where the fear comes from

Almost everything most of us have seen labelled "hypnosis" is stage entertainment — a performer, a row of
volunteers, and someone clucking like a chicken. It is a genuinely misleading picture, and it has done
decades of quiet damage to a serious practice.

What the stage show leaves out is selection. Volunteers are chosen precisely because they are highly
suggestible, extroverted, and — crucially — because they *want* to perform. They have walked onto a stage
in front of an audience. The compliance you're watching was there before the induction started.

Take away the stage, the audience, and the wish to entertain, and what remains is something much quieter.

## What the state actually feels like

The state used in this work is deep relaxation — close to the minutes just before you fall asleep, or the
way a long drive on a familiar road can end with you at your destination without much memory of the
journey. You have been in something like it many times without naming it.

In practice, during a session you can:

- hear everything I say, and answer in your own voice
- shift position, adjust the blanket, ask for water, or stop
- disagree with me, correct me, or decline to answer a question
- open your eyes and end the session at any point

Most people describe it as a daydream they can steer. You are not unconscious. You are not "under."
There is no moment where you hand something over.

> Hypnosis cannot make you act against your will. That is stage-show fiction, and letting go of it is
> usually the thing that lets someone finally book.

## What "control" really means here

There's a deeper version of the worry, and it's worth naming: not *will I be made to act*, but *will
something come out of me that I didn't choose to share*.

Here the honest answer is that you remain the one deciding. People routinely reach material in a session
and choose not to describe it in detail, and that is a complete and acceptable answer. We can look at
something from a distance, move past it, or stop entirely. You set the pace throughout — that isn't a
courtesy, it's how the method works.

It is also worth saying plainly: sessions are strictly one-on-one, and everything said in them is
confidential. Your session, your recording, your story.

## What this doesn't replace

Hypnotherapy and coaching are complementary practices. They are not a substitute for medical or mental
health care, and nothing here is a diagnosis or a treatment claim. If you're in acute crisis, please
start with a licensed professional or your local crisis line — this work will still be here afterwards.
